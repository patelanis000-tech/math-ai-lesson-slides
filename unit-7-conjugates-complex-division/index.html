02 � Re and Im � 7-10 minutes

The imaginary part is a real number

In z=a+bi, the two real numbers a and b tell us where the point lies.

Re(z)=a,Im(z)=b

Re names the real part. Im names the coefficient of i, not the whole term bi.

Worked example

Re(3+2i)=3 Im(3+2i)=2

The imaginary part is 2, not 2i.

Individual check

For z=-4-5i, write Re(z) and Im(z). What changes for z=7i?

Re(-4-5i)=-4 Im(-4-5i)=-5

For 7i, the real part is 0 and the imaginary part is 7.

03 � Conjugate notation � 10-12 minutes

Conjugates change the sign of the imaginary part

The complex conjugate of z=a+bi is written z*. It keeps the real part and reverses the imaginary part.

z=a+bi?z*=a-bi

Worked example

z=3+2i?z*=3-2i

Some sources use a bar over z for the same conjugate.

Individual check

Write the conjugates of 1-4i, 2i and 5. Which of these stays unchanged?

1+4i,-2i,5

A real number has imaginary part 0, so it is its own conjugate.

04 � Argand reflection � 12-17 minutes

Reflection and a half-turn are different

Conjugation reflects a point across the real axis. Negation reverses both coordinates.

z=3+2i z*=3-2ibut-z=-3-2i

z* stays on the same vertical line as z. The point -z is reached by a half-turn about the origin.

For z=-2+3i, sketch z* and -z. Label each point.
z*=-2-3i is the reflected point; -z=2-3i is the half-turn point.

The real axis is the mirror for conjugation.

07 � Cartesian division � explanation � 30-33 minutes

Make a real denominator without changing the value

Division becomes easier when the denominator is real. Multiply by its conjugate to make that happen.

For a nonzero denominator w:

w*w*=1 zw=zw�w*w*=zw*|w|2

We multiply both numerator and denominator by the same nonzero number, so the quotient keeps its value.

A familiar idea

This is like rationalising a surd denominator: use a conjugate pair to replace the denominator with something simpler.

Here the product ww*=|w|2 is a positive real number because w?0.

For a denominator 1-i, which factor would you multiply both top and bottom by?
Use 1+i, the conjugate of 1-i.

12 � Cis division � explanation � 45-48 minutes

Division undoes scaling and rotation

Multiplying moduli and adding angles was our rule for multiplication. Division reverses those actions.

For r2>0:

A negative denominator angle therefore produces an anticlockwise undoing turn.
