Where did this come from?
\(\dfrac{dP}{dt}=kP\)
Exponential growth says the rate of change is proportional to how much is already present.
But it contains no built-in reason to slow down.
exponential model
Verhulst's idea
\(\text{growth rate}\propto P\left(1-\dfrac{P}{L}\right)\)
\(P\)
how much is already present
\(1-P/L\)
the fraction of capacity still available
Think If \(P\) is close to \(L\), what happens to the second factor?
It becomes close to zero, so the growth rate becomes small. That gives the model its slowing behaviour near capacity.
The logistic family
Solving that differential equation leads to
\(P(t)=\dfrac{L}{1+Ce^{-kt}},\qquad L,C,k>0\)
We will not derive it today. We will learn to read it, manipulate it with technology, and interpret it.
