How confident are you?

Growth: \(b>1\). Decay: \(0<b<1\). Logarithms reverse exponentiation. \(A_0\) is the initial amount; \(b\) is the multiplier per time unit. Exponential decay approaches 0 without reaching it in the model.

Repeated multiplicative decay

Each interval keeps one half

\(1\;\to\;\frac12\;\to\;\frac14\;\to\;\frac18\;\to\;\frac1{16}\)

Meaning

Equal time intervals create equal ratios, not equal decreases.

Invariant

For exponential decay, half-life is constant and does not depend on the initial amount.

Two equivalent forms

Choose the form that fits the information

Multiplier form

\[A(t)=A_0b^t\]

For \(0<b<1\):

\[t_{1/2}=\frac{\ln(0.5)}{\ln(b)}\]

Continuous-rate form

\[A(t)=A_0e^{-kt}\]

For \(k>0\):

\[t_{1/2}=\frac{\ln 2}{k}\]
Predict before calculating

When will half remain?

\[A(t)=800(0.92)^t\]

Will the half-life be closer to 5, 10 or 20 time units? Explain your estimate.

Worked example

Make “half” an equation

Half of 800 is 400:

\[800(0.92)^t=400\]
\[(0.92)^t=0.5\]
\[t=\frac{\ln(0.5)}{\ln(0.92)}\approx 8.31\]

Interpretation: the modelled amount halves every 8.31 time units, regardless of the starting amount.

Unfamiliar attempt · predict first

Write the model in \(e^{-kt}\) form

A tracer begins at 450 mg and has a half-life of 11 days.

Find \(k\), write a model, then explain what the model predicts after 22 days.

\[k=\frac{\ln2}{11}\approx0.0630,\qquad A(t)=450e^{-0.0630t}\]

After 22 days, two half-lives have passed, so about \(450(\tfrac12)^2=112.5\) mg remains.

A slow-growth model

A skill score is modelled by

\[S(t)=12+5\ln(t+1),\qquad t\ge0\]

\(t\): weeks of practice · \(S\): score points

Before calculating

Growth continues, but slows

\(S(0)=12\)

Solve and defend the domain

When does the model reach 25?

\[12+5\ln(t+1)=25\]

Undo each operation. Then state both mathematical and contextual domains.

\[\ln(t+1)=2.6\]
\[t=e^{2.6}-1\approx12.5\]

Mathematical domain: \(t>-1\). Contextual domain: \(t\ge0\), restricted further to the period for which practice data make the model credible.

Long term: logarithmic growth is unbounded but progressively slower; exponential growth accelerates and eventually dominates.

Do not confuse these

Model family vs transformation

Natural-logarithmic model

\[y=a+b\ln(x)\]

The logarithm is part of the model itself. The original \(y\) changes slowly as \(x\) grows.

Logarithmic transformation

\[y=ab^x\Rightarrow \ln y=\ln a+x\ln b\]

We transform another model to reveal a linear relationship and estimate its parameters.

Gate 1 · predict first

Where does 50 belong?

Ordinary logarithmic axisbase 10 · positive values only
Horizontal axis: quantity (log scale), domain \(1\le x\le10000\)

Is 50 halfway between 10 and 100? What does equal spacing mean here?

Gate 1 · interpretation

Equal distances represent equal ratios

Spacing

\(1,10,100,1000,10000\) are equally spaced because each step multiplies by 10.

50 is not halfway

The midpoint between 10 and 100 on a log axis is \(\sqrt{10\cdot100}\approx31.6\).

Restriction

Zero and negative values cannot appear on an ordinary logarithmic axis.

Gate 2 · predict the transformation

How can a curve become a line?

\[y=ab^x\]

Take natural logs of both sides. Which parameter will control the gradient?

Semi-log linearisation

Plot \(\ln(y)\) against \(x\)

\[\ln(y)=\ln(a)+x\ln(b)\]

Straight-line form

\[Y=c+mx\]

\(Y=\ln y\), \(c=\ln a\), \(m=\ln b\)

Recover the model

\[a=e^{\text{intercept}},\qquad b=e^{\text{gradient}}\]

What does straightness suggest?

Evidence, not automatic validity

Mathematical evidence

The transformed points are approximately linear, with gradient \(0.438\) and intercept \(3.178\).

\[a=e^{3.178}\approx24.0,\qquad b=e^{0.438}\approx1.55\]

Contextual judgement

A possible model is \(y=24(1.55)^x\), but straightness alone does not prove unlimited growth, causal validity, or safe extrapolation.

Common algebraic trap

The written angle is not automatically the shift

\[a\sin(bx-\varphi)+d=a\sin\!\left(b\left(x-\frac{\varphi}{b}\right)\right)+d\]

Do not say

“The phase shift is \(\varphi\)” automatically.

Instead

Factor \(b\). The phase shift is \(\varphi/b\), with units matching \(x\).

Boundary and continuity

Do the rules meet at the switch?

From the first rule

\[C(4)=8\]

From the second expression

\[8+1.5(4-4)=8\]
The pieces meet at \((4,8)\), so this model is continuous at the switch. Continuity is not required for every piecewise model.
Model judgement

Why use more than one rule?

Different mechanisms

A flat base charge and a distance charge describe different regimes.

Contextual domain

Use \(0<d\le d_{\max}\), where \(d_{\max}\) is the service boundary.

Possible jumps

Tiered prices or entry fees may create discontinuities. That can be contextually correct.

Mixed HL modelling synthesis

Six outcomes · one modelling habit

Use mathematics and context together. A fitted pattern is never the whole decision.

6 · Justify a model

A transformed plot is nearly straight, but residuals grow at the largest values and the mechanism must eventually saturate.

Should you extrapolate far beyond the data? Justify with mathematical and contextual evidence.

Solutions · 1 to 3

Check the mathematics and the sentence

1. \(t_{1/2}=\ln(0.5)/\ln(0.87)\approx4.98\). The modelled mass halves every 4.98 time units.

2. \(R(8)=9+4\ln9\approx17.8\). Growth continues without bound but slows progressively.

3. \(a=e^{2.10}\approx8.17\), \(b=e^{0.27}\approx1.31\). The transform supports an exponential model only within a contextually defensible domain.

Solutions · 4 to 6

Interpret the structure

4. At \(t=4\), \(H=7\) and the model crosses the principal axis upward. Period \(=2\pi/(\pi/6)=12\) time units.

5. \(F(8)=6+2(5)=16\). Both pieces give 6 at the switch, so the model is continuous at \(d=3\).

6. Do not extrapolate far. Increasing residuals weaken the fit, while the saturation mechanism contradicts unlimited continuation of the transformed linear pattern.
