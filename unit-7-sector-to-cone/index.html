Domain: what values of α make a real, non-flat cone? Explain both endpoints.
Check the domain

0° < α < 360°. At 0° there is no base; at 360° the height is zero. Neither endpoint gives a usable cone.

58–69 min · Two pairs join

A cone must hold 600 mL

Keep the sector radius at 12 cm. The open cone must hold at least 600 mL. Use as little card as possible.

  1. Use the graph or table to find two sector angles with capacity about 600 cm³. Compare their curved areas.
  2. Choose a whole-degree angle that meets the capacity requirement. Check it and the next smaller whole-degree angle.
  3. Give your recommendation with capacity, card area and one limitation of the model.

V = (π/3)(α/30)²√(144 − (α/30)²)   ·   A = 0.4πα

1 mL = 1 cm³. Ignore thickness and overlap initially. Rotate calculator, diagram, recording and checking roles.

SL and HL · Compare recommendations

Which design uses less card?

The 600 cm³ intersections are approximately 240.2° and 331.2°. The smaller angle uses less card.

Choose 241°: V ≈ 602.4 cm³ and A ≈ 302.8 cm². At 240°, V ≈ 599.5 cm³, so it is too small.

Real seams and card thickness reduce capacity. A practical design needs extra allowance and a physical fill test.

Would the greatest-volume cone also use the least card? Explain using your results.

A silo

A silo is a cylinder of radius 3 m and height 8 m, topped by a hemisphere of radius 3 m. Model the walls as having negligible thickness.

  1. Find the internal volume.
  2. Find the external area painted above ground.
  3. Paint covers 12 m² per litre per coat. For two coats, how many 5 L tins must be bought? Assume no wastage.
Sketch and shade only the painted surfaces. Explain whether the shared circle or floor belongs in your calculation.
Compare expressions

Silo solution

Volume

π(3²)(8) + 23π(3³) = 90π ≈ 282.7 m³

Painted area

2π(3)(8) + 2π(3²) = 66π ≈ 207.3 m²

Shared and hidden circles are not exposed surfaces.

Check the purchase

2 × 66π ÷ 12 ≈ 34.6 L. Buy 7 tins (35 L). Rounding down to 6 tins leaves too little paint.
