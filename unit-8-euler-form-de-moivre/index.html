MATHS AI · HL · EULER FORM & POWERS

Teacher notes

Lesson sequence

Begin with the six oral questions, asking each student to explain one answer. Connect the powers prediction to the cis multiplication rule from the previous lesson.

Introduce Euler's formula, then let students work through conversions independently. Use the equivalent-arguments example to discuss the principal interval.

Develop de Moivre's theorem from repeated multiplication. Use the Argand diagram to connect the algebra to distance and direction.

Points to check

Students should raise the modulus to the power and multiply the argument by the power. A final argument may need adjusting by a multiple of 2π.

Check zero and negative powers before independent practice. Discuss selected solutions as a whole class.

The calculator pages are references. All examples use radians, and exact working should support the final answers.

Calculator instructions were checked against TI documentation; they have not been tested on a handheld or emulator.

TI-84 Plus / CE

Mode. Press MODE. Choose Radian, then a+bi for Cartesian output or re(θi) for exponential output. Press 2nd → MODE (QUIT).

Entry. For 2eiπ/3, enter 2 × e^((π ÷ 3) × i). Use 2nd → ln for e-to-a-power, 2nd → ^ for π, and 2nd → . for i. In MathPrint, right-arrow out of the exponent before continuing.

Convert. Enter (3+4i), then MATH → CPX → 7: ►Polar → ENTER. Expected result: 5e^(0.927295218i). Use CPX → 6: ►Rect for the reverse.

Power. Put the entire complex base in parentheses before its exponent. ►Polar and ►Rect are end-of-expression commands for complex results; do not apply them to a purely real result such as −8. Degree mode causes Domain error for complex exponentials.

TI-84 guidebook, pp. 51–56 · TI-84 complex exponential support

TI-Nspire CX / CX II

Mode. Home → Settings → Document Settings (Home 5, then 2). Set Angle: Radian and Real or Complex: Rectangular or Polar, then OK. Do not use Exponential Format; it controls Normal, Scientific or Engineering display.

Entry. On a Calculator page, use the dedicated ex key and the π-symbol palette for actual π and i (alphabetic e and i are variables). Enter 2×e^((π÷3)×i); right-arrow out of the exponent.

Convert. Enter (3+4i), then MENU → Number → Complex Number Tools → Convert to Polar. Use Convert to Rectangular in the same submenu. Radian polar output is reiθ; degree output is r∠θ.

Power. Use parentheses around the full base. Ctrl+Enter requests approximate output. Degree or Gradian mode causes Domain error for complex exponentials.

Exact radical and π display varies by model. The documented steps are not handheld- or emulator-tested. TI-Nspire settings and conversions · π-symbol palette
