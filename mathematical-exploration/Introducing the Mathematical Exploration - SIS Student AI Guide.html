STUDENT GUIDE

Academic Integrity and Responsible AI Use

Diploma Programme and Career-related Programme

Internally and externally assessed work, including IA, TOK, EE and Reflective Project

Quick guide Student responsibilities FAQs Declaration Logbook

Core principle

Use AI to help you find, question and understand information - not to replace your reading, thinking, decision-making or authorship. Disclose your use, verify original sources, cite appropriately and make sure the final work is genuinely your own.

Quick guide: Green, Amber, Red

GREEN

  • Generate search terms or possible sources, then find and read the originals.
  • Use AI summaries to help you understand a source, then verify the source yourself.
  • Use AI to identify alternative viewpoints, then investigate and evaluate them.
  • Use basic spelling or grammar tools where permitted by the subject.
  • Keep drafts, notes, version history and records of permitted AI use.

AMBER - ASK FIRST

  • Use AI to generate or refine a research question.
  • Ask AI to comment on or grade a formal IB assessment draft.
  • Use AI to create an outline or structure for assessed work.
  • Upload assessed drafts, teacher feedback, personal data or school documents into an AI tool.
  • Use translation or language-support tools in assessed work, especially language acquisition.

RED - NOT ACCEPTABLE

  • Submit AI-generated text, images, code, data, analysis or ideas as your own.
  • Hide AI use or make a false declaration.
  • Use AI to rewrite or materially reshape assessed work.
  • Use AI to generate personal reflections, conclusions or evaluation that should be your own.
  • Use or cite references that you have not opened, read and verified.

Amber means the use may depend on the task, subject guide, teacher instructions, privacy rules or the type of assistance provided.

What every student must do

  • Produce authentic work that reflects your own knowledge, skills, decisions and achievement.
  • Follow the instructions for the specific subject and assessment. A use permitted in class may not be permitted in final assessed work.
  • Disclose AI use honestly in the Student Declaration of AI Use.
  • Keep an AI Logbook whenever you use AI in the production of assessed work.
  • Where AI-generated material is incorporated into the work, it must be cited at the point of use and included in the reference list.
  • Verify original sources. Never rely on AI-generated quotations, facts or references without checking them.
  • Keep process evidence such as notes, plans, drafts, source records and version history.
  • Be able to explain your choices, reasoning, methods, sources and development of the work.

Acknowledgement, citation and the logbook are different

Declaration / acknowledgement

Tells the reader whether you used AI, which tool you used, at what stage and for what purpose. Include enough detail, including the relevant prompt or prompts, to explain how the tool was used and what it contributed.

Point-of-use citation and reference

Credits specific AI-generated words, images, code, data or other material included in the work. Cite it at the point of use and include it in the reference list. The reference must identify the AI tool and include the prompt used and the date the material was generated.

AI Logbook

Records each use during the process: date, tool, what you asked and what you did with the output. This is an internal school record.

Important

The declaration and logbook are SIS requirements. They support transparency and authentication. They do not make an otherwise prohibited use acceptable.

What happens if there is a concern?

  1. The teacher identifies the specific concern. A Turnitin AI-writing indicator above 20% prompts a preliminary review under SIS procedure, but the score is not proof of misconduct.
  2. You provide your declaration, logbook and relevant process evidence.
  3. The teacher explains the concern and gives you a fair opportunity to explain your work and AI use.
  4. The teacher may consult the DP/CP Coordinator or another teacher.
  5. The concern may be resolved, more clarification may be requested, or the matter may move to a formal investigation under the school policy.
  6. If academic misconduct is confirmed, the school Academic Integrity Policy applies. Please refer to the policy for details.

A detector can produce what is known as a false positive.

An automated result can identify authentic writing as AI-generated. Teachers therefore consider the score together with your process evidence, subject-specific work and explanation. No score proves misconduct by itself.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use AI for brainstorming?

Usually, but only when your teacher permits it. You remain responsible for selecting, developing and evaluating the ideas. Record the use in your logbook and declaration.

Can AI create my research question?

AI may provide examples or prompts for discussion, but it must not independently determine your final research question. Your final question should emerge from your own inquiry and teacher-approved process.

Can I use AI to find sources?

AI may suggest search terms or possible sources. You must open, read, evaluate and cite the original source. Do not cite an AI summary instead of the original author.

Can AI summarize a source?

It may help you understand a source, but you must read and verify the source yourself. Your final interpretation and writing must be your own.

Can AI make an outline?

Only where your teacher permits it. A general structure may be acceptable when disclosed, but AI must not determine your argument, evidence, analysis, conclusions or reflections.

Can AI proofread my work?

Basic spelling and grammar support may be allowed in some subjects. It may not be allowed where language is being assessed, especially in language acquisition. Ask your teacher before using it.

Can AI translate my assessed work?

Do not use AI to translate assessed work into the submission language. This can replace the language performance that the assessment is intended to measure.

Can AI give feedback on my draft?

Ask your teacher first. Detailed, repeated or rubric-based AI feedback may shape the work beyond permitted support and may conflict with subject-specific feedback limits.

Can I use NotebookLM?

NotebookLM and similar source-grounded systems are treated as AI tools under this framework. Their use is subject to the same requirements for teacher permission, source verification, disclosure, citation and retention of process evidence.

Do I need a logbook if I did not use AI?

No. Select “I did not use AI tools” in the declaration. Do not create a logbook entry for ordinary non-AI software unless your teacher instructs you to do so.

What should I do if I used AI but forgot to record it?

Tell your teacher promptly and reconstruct the record honestly from available history. Do not hide the use or invent details.

Can I use a humanizer to lower a detector score?

No. Do not use software to disguise AI-generated text or to manipulate a detector. Authentic work should not be rewritten solely to reduce an automated score.

Logbook reminders

  • Add entries during the process, not only at the end.
  • Record what you actually did with the output, including when you rejected or did not use it.
  • The logbook must match your declaration and any citations in the work.
  • A logbook does not make prohibited AI use acceptable.
  • Provide the logbook, relevant history, drafts or version records when requested.
