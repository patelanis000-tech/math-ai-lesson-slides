UNIT-CIRCLE DEFINITION · 2 MINUTES

The coordinates name the direction

For an acute angle θ, your unit vector has components cos θ and sin θ. The familiar triangle ratios now give the coordinates directly because its length is 1.

A unit circle has centre (0, 0) and radius 1. We extend the meaning of sine and cosine to every directed angle by defining

P = (cos θ, sin θ).

Measure θ from the positive x-axis: anticlockwise is positive; clockwise is negative. A coordinate is signed even though a triangle side length is not.

REFERENCE

Keep these connections together

Coordinates and angle

P = (cos θ, sin θ)

θ is measured from the positive x-axis. Positive turns are anticlockwise. Adding 2π gives the same point.

Gradient and magnitude

tan θ = sin θ / cos θ
cos²θ + sin²θ = 1

Tangent is undefined when cos θ = 0. Both coordinates, not tangent alone, identify a unit direction.

From rotation to graphs

Sine records the vertical coordinate; cosine records the horizontal coordinate. Both have range [−1,1] and period 2π.

cos θ = sin(θ + π/2)

From direction to a vector

v = r(cos θ, sin θ),   r > 0

Uniform scaling changes magnitude. Rotation changes direction. A quarter-turn sends (x,y) to (−y,x).

The unit circle gives the geometric foundation. Graphical trigonometric equations are a separate next step.
