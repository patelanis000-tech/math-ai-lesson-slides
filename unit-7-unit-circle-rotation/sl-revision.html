Explain the boundary

Moving an anchor closer to A across the ground forces a greater vertical rise if the cable length remains fixed.

The angle restriction prevents the planar distance becoming too small. The wall’s upper y-boundary limits movement in the other direction.

Hint: separate height from the third side

For area, use the perpendicular height associated with the two given sides. For perimeter, the third side must be calculated separately. Draw the two included angles to compare the heights.

Check the two decisions

Both areas are ½(8)(13)sin 53° = ½(8)(13)sin 127° ≈ 41.5 cm².

The third sides are √(233 − 208cos 53°) ≈ 10.4 cm and √(233 − 208cos 127°) ≈ 18.9 cm. The perimeters are approximately 31.4 cm and 39.9 cm.

Only panel A meets both conditions. The heights are equal, but changing the included angle changes the third side and hence the perimeter.

Compare possible constructions

One larger-area pair uses 70° and 110°: each area is approximately 48.9 cm². One smaller-area pair uses 30° and 150°: each area is 26 cm². Many other pairs are possible.

Supplementary included angles give equal perpendicular heights, and therefore equal areas for the fixed sides. The maximum area is 52 cm² when the sides are perpendicular: the full 13 cm side becomes the height to the 8 cm base.

Check the proposed anchor

The coordinate changes are (3,4,12). The planar distance is 5 m, the cable length is √(9+16+144) = 13 m, and the elevation is tan⁻¹(12/5) ≈ 67.4°.

The proposed anchor uses the full cable and lies on the allowed wall, but the elevation exceeds 60°. It is not acceptable.

3² + y² + (z − 1)² = 169.

The vertical rise cannot exceed 13sin 60°. Remember that the rise is measured from height 1, not from ground level.

Compare two valid anchors

Two examples are B₁ = (3, √60, 11) and B₂ = (3, √96, 9).

For B₁, the squared length is 9+60+100 = 169; y ≈ 7.75 is within the wall. The elevation is sin⁻¹(10/13) ≈ 50.3°.

For B₂, the squared length is 9+96+64 = 169; y ≈ 9.80 is within the wall. The elevation is sin⁻¹(8/13) ≈ 38.0°.

Both use the full cable and satisfy every stated restriction.

Check the complete range

At the limiting elevation of 60°, the planar distance is 13cos 60° = 6.5 m. Since the planar distance squared is 3²+y², the smallest y is √33.25 ≈ 5.77 m.

The maximum rise is 13sin 60°, so the greatest height is 1+13sin 60° ≈ 12.3 m.

B = (3, y, 1 + √(160 − y²)),
√33.25 ≤ y ≤ 10.

The positive square root keeps the anchor above A. Both endpoints are allowed because the angle and wall restrictions are inclusive.

Hint: begin with the plan view

The interior angle at A is 50° and the interior angle at B is 40°. C is therefore a right angle. Use the sine rule for AC and BC, then resolve AC into east and north components.

Check the survey

AC = 600sin 40° ≈ 385.7 m and BC = 600sin 50° ≈ 459.6 m.

C ≈ (247.906, 295.442), while M = (300,0). Thus MC = √((247.906−300)²+295.442²) = 300 m, using unrounded coordinates.

Alternatively, the midpoint of the hypotenuse of a right triangle is equally distant from its three vertices, so MC = 300 m.

Check the camera recommendation

At A, the straight-line distance is √((600sin 40°)²+150²) ≈ 414 m, and the elevation is approximately 21.3°. The angle is acceptable, but the range is not.

At M, the distance is √(300²+150²) ≈ 335 m and the elevation is tan⁻¹(150/300) ≈ 26.6°. M satisfies both limits.

At M the angle restriction gives 300tan 20° ≤ h ≤ 300tan 30°, approximately 109 m to 173 m. The range restriction gives h ≤ √(400²−300²) ≈ 265 m, so it is less restrictive. The acceptable heights are approximately 109–173 m, with boundaries included.

Check after both decisions are complete

Panel: Both areas are 70sin 40° ≈ 45.0 cm². The third sides are approximately 9.03 cm and 22.6 cm, giving perimeters of 33.0 cm and 46.6 cm. Only the 40° panel satisfies both limits.

Cable: The changes are (6,8,12); the planar distance is 10 m. The length is √244 ≈ 15.6 m, but the angle is tan⁻¹(12/10) ≈ 50.2°, so the proposed anchor fails the angle condition.

At the 45° limit the rise can be at most 10 m, so the greatest anchor height is 11 m. The cable then has length √200 ≈ 14.1 m, within its 16 m limit.
